# Beyond Human Emotion

**Emotion as a Cross-System Paradigm**  
*Emotional Pluralism, Affective Non-Equivalence, and the Problem of Understanding Other Minds*

An independent philosophical proposal about how to understand emotion across different kinds of beings, including animals and hypothetical future artificial intelligences.

## Central idea

Human emotion words are valuable but should not be presumed to exhaust every possible emotional repertoire. Similar behavior does not establish identical emotion; different biological or artificial construction does not establish different emotion. Comparisons must be justified, and new categories should be introduced only when they explain meaningful, recurring distinctions.

The manuscript develops:

- **Emotional Pluralism:** emotional repertoires may overlap, converge, or diverge across subjects.
- **The Principle of Affective Non-Equivalence:** partial resemblance never automatically proves complete equivalence, and unfamiliarity never automatically proves deficiency.
- **Contrastive Emotional Interpretation:** a practical method for comparing recognized emotions and deciding when a distinct category is warranted.
- **Interpretive humility:** acknowledging and correcting for the human standpoint rather than pretending we can escape it.
- **An ethical bridge:** separating emotional recognition from claims about welfare interests, moral obligations, or rights.

## Scope

This is **not** an argument that today's AI has consciousness or feelings. Artificial emotion is considered *conditionally*: if an artificial being is independently established as genuinely emotional, how should its states be interpreted? The imagined artificial emotions in the manuscript are illustrative placeholders, not observed phenomena.

The work is an independent conceptual theory, not a claim of experimental validation. Its references identify relevant existing research and philosophy; their inclusion does not imply that the cited authors endorse the proposal.
